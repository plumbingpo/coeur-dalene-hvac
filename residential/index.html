Proper Sizing

Equipment Sized for Your Actual House

When it comes to HVAC, bigger is not better. An oversized unit short-cycles, wastes energy, and never runs long enough to pull humidity out of the air, while an undersized one runs nonstop and still can't keep up on the hottest days. That's why we never just copy the tonnage of the old unit. We run a proper load calculation that factors in your square footage, insulation, windows, and ductwork, and for mini-splits we size each head to the room it serves.

Choosing new equipment comes down to more than a SEER2 number. Our technician will compare heat pumps against a furnace-and-AC pair, and ducted against ductless, laying out operating costs, electrical and venting requirements, and the installation itself. Growing families often do best with two-stage or variable-speed equipment, which holds tighter temperatures from room to room and uses less energy. You choose what fits your home and budget.

Common Problems

Common HVAC Problems We Fix in Coeur d'Alene

No cooling, a furnace that refuses to light, ice on the indoor coil, a slow refrigerant leak: every one of these problems has a different cause and needs its own repair. Coeur d'Alene HVAC tracks down the exact fault before replacing anything, whether it's a condenser that hums without starting, a furnace that short-cycles, a thermostat that loses its settings, a clogged condensate drain, or a mini-split throwing error codes.

Not every HVAC problem is equally urgent. A bad capacitor only keeps the compressor from starting. A cracked heat exchanger, on the other hand, can let combustion gases into the house, and a backed-up condensate drain can soak through a ceiling before anyone notices. We come prepared to swap capacitors, contactors, fan motors, igniters, flame sensors, and control boards, seal ductwork that's leaking air into the attic, and recharge refrigerant once a leak is fixed.
