Complete Heating & Cooling Services for Boise Properties

Heating and cooling around here means a wide mix of equipment: older gas furnaces on the older streets, newer heat pumps in recent construction, ductless mini-splits in additions and converted garages, and rooftop units on commercial buildings. Our team handles all of it. Our technicians have years in the field, and we've learned how the local climate, the age of local homes, and local code affect the way equipment performs over time.

Most homeowners we work with need one of three things: a repair, a replacement, or a seasonal tune-up. Repairs are usually quick: a burned contactor, a dirty flame sensor, a slow refrigerant leak, or a seized blower motor. When we replace a system, it gets a proper load calculation, not a guess based on the old unit's tonnage, which keeps temperatures even and stops the new equipment from short-cycling its way to an early failure. Maintenance visits include coil cleaning, a refrigerant charge check, safety control testing, and a static pressure reading, and nothing does more to extend equipment life.

Local businesses receive the same attention. Getting More Years From Your HVAC System in Boise

Most mid-season breakdowns trace back to skipped maintenance, and the habits that prevent them are easy. Swap your filter every one to three months, clear vegetation at least two feet from the outdoor condenser, and hose off the condenser coil once a year in spring. Then book a professional tune-up twice a year, one for cooling and one for heating, so our technicians can catch small problems while they're still cheap to fix.

Take a look at the age of your system. Its age says a lot about what comes next. Most well-maintained air conditioners make it to 12 to 15 years, furnaces 15 to 20, and heat pumps 10 to 15. If your system is beyond that range and is also showing rising bills, uneven temperatures, or frequent repairs, it's usually due for a planned replacement, which is almost always cheaper and easier on everyone than an emergency replacement in peak season.

Minor issues don't stay minor when they're ignored. Consider a weak capacitor: swapping it out is a short, affordable repair, but if it's ignored for a season, it can burn out the compressor, and suddenly a minor fix becomes a full system replacement. That's why we encourage homeowners to save our number in their phone and reach out at the first hint of trouble. Catching it early saves time and money and prevents a miserable night.
